Privacy Policy

Last updated {{ lastUpdated }}

Bulwriter is a small, independently-run project — there's no company behind it, just one person, based in the United Kingdom, building an open-source screenwriting tool. This policy explains what information Bulwriter collects when you use it, why, and what your rights are over it. It's written in plain language on purpose; where it references specific laws, that's UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018, which apply regardless of where you're writing from if you're using this service.

What we collect

Account information

Sign-in is handled entirely by Clerk, a third-party authentication provider — Bulwriter's own servers never see or store your password. Clerk holds your email address, display name, and (if you set one) a profile picture. Bulwriter's database stores only your Clerk-issued user ID, used to link your account to your projects.

Content you create

Everything you write or enter — scripts, scenes, story bible notes, version history, and anything you fill into the production-planning tools (schedules, budgets, casting, crew, locations, and similar) — is stored in Bulwriter's database so it's there when you come back. This is your creative work; see the Terms of Service for how ownership works.

Collaboration data

If you invite someone to a project, we store the email address you invited and the role you gave them, so the invite can be matched to their account when they sign in.

Payment information

If you choose to donate, checkout is handled entirely by Stripe. Bulwriter's servers never receive or store your card number or other payment details — Stripe processes that directly. We only know that a checkout session was started, not whether it was completed.

Technical data

Like any web service, Bulwriter's servers process your IP address as part of handling requests (for example, to apply rate limits that protect the service from abuse). This isn't stored persistently or linked to your account beyond that immediate purpose.

Cookies and local storage

Clerk sets essential cookies needed to keep you signed in. Your browser's local storage is used for one thing: remembering whether you prefer light or dark mode. Bulwriter does not use advertising cookies, analytics trackers, or any third-party tracking scripts — there simply aren't any in the app.

Who we share it with

We don't sell your data, and we don't share it beyond what's needed to run the service. That means the processors named above — Clerk (authentication), Stripe (donations) — plus the infrastructure that hosts Bulwriter: Railway (database and backend) and Amazon Web Services (static frontend hosting, served from London, UK). Each of these is a global company that may process data outside the UK; where that happens, it's covered by their own standard contractual safeguards as data processors.

Bulwriter's AI-assisted features (like generating a story bible draft from an uploaded document, which would send that document's text to Anthropic's Claude API) are built into the codebase but not currently switched on for users. If and when that changes, this policy will be updated first.

How long we keep it

Deleted projects and scripts move to trash and are kept there for 30 days before being permanently purged, so accidental deletions are recoverable. If you'd like your account and its data deleted sooner or entirely, contact us (below) and we'll action it.

Your rights

Under UK GDPR, you have the right to access the data we hold about you, correct it, ask us to delete it, restrict or object to its processing, and receive a copy of it in a portable format. To exercise any of these, email us at the address below. If you're not satisfied with our response, you can complain to the UK's Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.

Children

Bulwriter isn't directed at children, and we don't knowingly collect data from anyone under 13.

Security

We take reasonable steps to protect your data, but no online service can guarantee perfect security. If you're storing work you can't afford to lose, we'd still recommend exporting a copy periodically — see the note on backups in the Terms of Service.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes in a material way, we'll update the date at the top of this page. Significant changes (for example, switching on a data-processing feature that's currently dormant) will be called out clearly, not buried in a diff.
